Efficacy of Technology-Augmented Behavioral Counseling for Risky Firearm Behaviors: A Randomized Clinical Trial.

JAMA Pediatr · Carter PM et al. · DOI

RCTMulticenterMental & behavioral healthOther
Clinical 10/10 Research 6/10
Why it matters
Firearm violence is a leading cause of morbidity in adolescents and emerging adults; this trial provides evidence for an ED-based intervention that reduces risky firearm behaviors and associated violence.
Design
Prospective, 2-arm randomized clinical trial at 2 US trauma center EDs, enrolling 373 patients aged 16-30 with past 3-month firearm carriage.
Key findings
IntERact participants showed significantly greater reductions in risky firearm behaviors at 6 months (RR=0.51, 95% CI 0.33-0.79) and in physical violence at 3 months (RR=0.46, 95% CI 0.26-0.81) compared to enhanced usual care.
Bottom line
This ED-initiated, technology-augmented counseling intervention is promising and should be considered for implementation in trauma center EDs serving at-risk youth.
Why these scores

Clinical: Definitive multicenter RCT showing a technology-augmented counseling intervention reduces risky firearm behaviors and violence in ED patients, directly informing ED-based violence prevention practice.

Research: Standard RCT design applied to a novel intervention for a known problem, with strong methodology and clear implications for implementation research.

Pediatrics · Gable C et al. · DOI

Cross-sectionalMental & behavioral healthED operations & qualityEquity & access
Clinical 5/10 Research 5/10
Why it matters
Emergency holds are common in pediatric mental health ED visits, increasing over time, and linked to restraint use and longer stays.
Design
Retrospective cross-sectional study of 14,883 mental/behavioral health ED visits by patients aged 6–18 at one urban tertiary pediatric ED, 2019–2024.
Key findings
2,219 visits (15%) arrived under emergency hold; among these, 87% were Black, 85% publicly insured, 20% came via mobile crisis, and 64% with law enforcement. Holds rose yearly and were associated with Black race, male sex, prior MBH visits, longer stays, and restraint use. Bottom line
Emergency holds are frequent and increasing; mobile crisis may mitigate restraint use, but further study is needed.

Methods: Single-center retrospective cross-sectional study using EHR data and multivariable generalized estimating equations.

Why these scores

Clinical: Single-center descriptive study of emergency holds and restraint use; provides background for ED mental health care but no direct practice change.

Research: Standard retrospective cross-sectional design applied to an understudied pediatric ED mental health population; suggests follow-up on mobile crisis impact.

Reassessing the Kocher Criteria: A Prospective Evaluation of Diagnostic Performance in Pediatric Musculoskeletal Infection.

J Pediatr Orthop · Rogie GA et al. · DOI

Prospective cohortInfection & feverOther
Clinical 6/10 Research 5/10
Why it matters
The Kocher criteria are widely used but have shown inconsistent performance and were not designed to distinguish septic arthritis from osteomyelitis.
Design
Prospective cohort study of 102 children aged 0 to 18 years with suspected musculoskeletal infection, comparing transient synovitis, osteomyelitis, and septic arthritis.
Key findings
CRP and ESR were significantly higher in septic arthritis and osteomyelitis than in transient synovitis (both P<0.001); WBC was weaker (P=0.041). For septic arthritis vs transient synovitis, probability rose from 9.5% with 0 criteria to 65% with 2, then plateaued at 66.7% with 3 or 4. With osteomyelitis included, overlap was substantial: among patients meeting 2 criteria, septic arthritis and osteomyelitis each accounted for 39.4%. Kocher burden did not differ between septic arthritis and osteomyelitis (P=0.874). In the hip subgroup, no septic arthritis or osteomyelitis occurred with 0 criteria.
Bottom line
The Kocher criteria are only moderately useful for septic arthritis vs transient synovitis and poorly differentiate septic arthritis from osteomyelitis; they should prompt MRI rather than a definitive surgical decision.

Methods: Prospective single-center cohort study (Level III) with 3 diagnostic groups.

Why these scores

Clinical: Prospective evidence that the Kocher criteria perform modestly for septic arthritis and poorly distinguish it from osteomyelitis, supporting use of MRI rather than a definitive surgical algorithm.

Research: Prospective single-center diagnostic study that extends a classic decision rule to include osteomyelitis overlap, though sample size and setting limit generalizability.

Cross-sectionalED operations & qualityEquity & access
Clinical 6/10 Research 5/10

Design
Retrospective cross-sectional study of 603 children triaged as Emergency Severity Index level 4 for abdominal pain at one urban pediatric ED during 2023.

Corticosteroid use in children with community acquired sepsis: a multicentre prospective cohort study.

Arch Dis Child · Long E et al. · DOI

Prospective cohortMulticenterInfection & feverResuscitation & critical care
Clinical 5/10 Research 6/10
Why it matters
Corticosteroid use in pediatric sepsis lacks strong evidence, and this large multicenter study reveals how commonly they are given in real-world emergency settings, highlighting a gap between practice and evidence that warrants clinical trials.
Design
Prospective observational cohort study using data from the Sepsis Epidemiology in Australian and New Zealand Emergency Departments study, enrolling children under 18 with suspected community-acquired sepsis across 11 EDs from 2021 to 2023.
Key findings
Among 5119 children (median age 1.6 years), in-hospital mortality was 1.0% (52/5119). Phoenix sepsis criteria were met by 4.9% (252/5119), with mortality 12.7% (32/252). Corticosteroids were given to 9.6% (489/5119) overall and 31.3% (79/252) of Phoenix-positive children. Dexamethasone was most common (69.5%, 340/489), then hydrocortisone (25.4%, 124/489). Use was associated with older age, multiple comorbidities, elevated lactate, large-volume fluids, and vasopressors; most use occurred outside refractory shock.
Bottom line
Corticosteroids are frequently administered to children with suspected community-acquired sepsis, often without evidence of fluid- or catecholamine-refractory shock, and agent choice varies widely by site.

Methods: Multicentre prospective cohort study (Australia/New Zealand EDs, 2021–2023).

Why these scores

Clinical: Describes current corticosteroid prescribing patterns in ED-admitted children with suspected sepsis; useful background but does not change practice.

Research: Multicentre prospective ED-based cohort describing corticosteroid use; standard methods but fills a descriptive gap and motivates trials.

Association of Child Opportunity Index domains with emergency department disposition: analysis of a statewide sample.

Acad Pediatr · Rojas CR et al. · DOI

Cross-sectionalMulticenterED operations & qualityEquity & access
Clinical 4/10 Research 6/10
Why it matters
This study helps clarify how neighborhood opportunity, measured by the Child Opportunity Index, relates to whether children are admitted or transferred from the ED, and which domain matters most.
Design
Cross-sectional analysis of all 2023 statewide pediatric ED encounters (<18 years), using multivariable logistic regression adjusted for insurance, race/ethnicity, age, diagnosis, medical complexity, and hospital type.
Key findings
Among 838,408 encounters, 26,579 (3.2%) were admitted and 18,130 (2.2%) transferred. There was a U-shaped association between admission/transfer and COI and each domain; associations varied by diagnosis group. The social and economic domain had the best relative model fit in most models.
Bottom line
Children in both the lowest and highest opportunity neighborhoods had higher odds of ED admission or transfer; social/economic factors were the most consistent predictor.

Methods: Statewide ED database + cross-sectional multivariable logistic regression

Why these scores

Clinical: Cross-sectional statewide analysis describes associations but does not change immediate ED decisions.

Research: Uses a composite neighborhood index to examine disposition, suggesting further work on which domains matter.

Reduced-lead electroencephalography performed at the discretion of emergency physicians in pediatric cases of altered mental status: A retrospective observational study.

Epilepsy Res · Yamaguchi H et al. · DOI

Retrospective cohortNeurology
Clinical 6/10 Research 6/10
Why it matters
Altered mental status in children is common and may reflect nonconvulsive seizures; a rapid, emergency physician–performed EEG could speed treatment decisions in the ED.
Design
This retrospective observational study reviewed medical records and reduced-lead EEGs from 51 pediatric ED visits for altered mental status with suspected electrographic seizures, with EEGs obtained at the discretion of emergency physicians.
Key findings
Anti-seizure medication was given during EEG monitoring in 74.1% of febrile seizure patients, 50.0% of acute encephalopathy/encephalitis patients, and 87.5% of epilepsy patients. Electrographic seizures were identified in 29.6%, 25.0%, and 93.8% of these groups, respectively. Seizures resolved during monitoring in all but one febrile seizure patient. Neurological sequelae occurred in 62.5% of encephalopathy/encephalitis patients and 6.3% of epilepsy patients, but none in febrile seizure patients.
Bottom line
Reduced-lead EEG appears feasible in the pediatric ED and may help guide timely seizure treatment, but whether this improves long-term outcomes remains unproven.

Methods: Retrospective observational study of ED medical records and reduced-lead EEG findings.

Why these scores

Clinical: Retrospective single-center study suggesting ED-performed reduced-lead EEG may guide seizure treatment, but outcome benefit unproven.

Research: Standard retrospective design with a novel application of ED-performed reduced-lead EEG; small sample limits rigor.
