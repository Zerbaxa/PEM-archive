Telemedicine Consultation for Pediatric Trauma in Community ED Visits and Parent Experience of Care: A Stepped-Wedge Cluster-Randomized Clinical Trial.

JAMA Netw Open · Marcin JP et al. · DOI

RCTMulticenterTrauma & injuryED operations & quality
Clinical 6/10 Research 9/10
Why it matters
Many injured children first present to nonpediatric EDs, and telemedicine could improve family experience during trauma consultation and transfer.
Design
Stepped-wedge cluster-randomized trial in 10 community EDs; 595 children with acute trauma received telemedicine or telephone-only pediatric trauma consultation.
Key findings
Overall parent experience (AMD 0.041), distress (AMD -0.031), and transfer odds (aOR 0.59) did not differ. Discharge/transfer experience subscale favored telemedicine (AMD 0.104).
Bottom line
Adding telemedicine did not change primary outcomes but improved transition-related experience; it is a reasonable adjunct while further research identifies which families benefit most.

Methods: Stepped-wedge cluster-randomized trial in 10 community EDs.

Why these scores

Clinical: RCT showed no overall difference in parent experience, distress, or transfer rates; only a subscale benefit in discharge/transfer experience, so practice change is unlikely based on this alone.

Research: A multicenter stepped-wedge cluster-randomized trial evaluating telemedicine for pediatric trauma consultations fills a clear evidence gap and tests a pragmatic ED intervention.

Reliability and Validity of the Australian Mental Health Triage Scale in Children's Hospitals.

Acad Emerg Med · Foster AA et al. · DOI

Prospective cohortMulticenterMental & behavioral healthED operations & quality
Clinical 6/10 Research 5/10
Why it matters
This study tests a mental health-specific triage tool in pediatric emergency departments, where behavioral health visits often cluster at the highest urgency level and may not be well differentiated by general triage scales.
Design
Prospective observational validation study at two U.S. children's hospitals; nurses assigned both the Emergency Severity Index and the Australian Mental Health Triage Scale, with some visits double-scored for reliability.
Key findings
Among 454 visits, 94.1% were ESI 2 but only 26.9% were AMHTS 2. Interrater reliability was fair (ICC 0.48, 95% CI 0.37–0.58). Higher-acuity AMHTS scores predicted greater resource use: score 1 had an IRR of 1.82 and score 2 an IRR of 1.64 versus score 5. Using an expanded resource definition, scores 1–4 all exceeded score 5.
Bottom line
The AMHTS showed fair reliability and good validity for predicting resource utilization, and it spread patients across more acuity levels than the ESI, suggesting it may better capture urgency in pediatric behavioral health emergencies.

Methods: Prospective observational validation study with paired triage scores at two children's hospital EDs.

Why these scores

Clinical: Provides validity and reliability data for a behavioral health triage scale in pediatric EDs, informing triage decisions without changing practice immediately.

Research: Prospective two-center validation of a triage scale in a pediatric behavioral health population; standard methods but fills a specific gap.

Prehospital Pediatric Ventilation Quality Following Use of a Feedback Device: A Randomized Study.

Pediatrics · Finney JD et al. · DOI

RCTMulticenterPrehospital & EMSResuscitation & critical care
Clinical 5/10 Research 5/10
Why it matters
Manual ventilation of children in respiratory arrest is often poorly performed by EMS clinicians, and this study shows how much room there is for improvement across multiple agencies.
Design
In a randomized crossover simulation, 123 EMS clinicians from five agencies ventilated a toddler manikin with and without real-time feedback from a ventilation feedback device.
Key findings
With feedback, guideline tidal volumes were achieved in 87% of breaths versus 16.2% without; rate adherence rose from 65% to 88.6%; mask leak under 30% occurred in 88.4% versus 80% of ventilations.
Bottom line
Real-time feedback substantially improved pediatric ventilation quality in simulation, suggesting it could help EMS clinicians better meet pediatric guidelines.

Methods: Simulation-based randomized crossover study across 5 US EMS agencies

Why these scores

Clinical: Randomized simulation evidence supports EMS decisions about using ventilation feedback devices, but real-patient confirmation is still needed.

Research: Multi-site randomized crossover simulation applies a standard method to an understudied prehospital pediatric ventilation question and invites real-patient follow-up.

Use of Intranasal Analgesia for Acute Pain in Pediatric Emergency Departments: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials.

Pain Res Manag · Alsabri M et al. · DOI

Systematic review / MASedation, analgesia & procedures
Clinical 7/10 Research 6/10
Why it matters
Acute pain is common in pediatric EDs, and choosing an intranasal analgesic requires comparative data on efficacy and safety.
Design
Systematic review and network meta-analysis of 15 randomized controlled trials involving 1,388 children in ED and prehospital settings.
Key findings
No intranasal or intravenous analgesic provided significantly greater pain reduction than intranasal fentanyl across timepoints; rescue analgesia rates were similar. Intranasal ketamine had more adverse events than standard-dose intranasal fentanyl, mostly mild and transient. Acceptability was high and ED length of stay did not differ meaningfully.
Bottom line
Intranasal fentanyl remains a well-supported first-line option for acute pediatric pain; intranasal ketamine should be used with attention to adverse effects.
Why these scores

Clinical: High-quality evidence synthesis supporting current ED analgesic choices and cautioning about intranasal ketamine adverse effects.

Research: Network meta-analysis of 15 RCTs comparing intranasal analgesics across routes and agents in pediatric acute pain.

Balanced Solutions Versus Normal Saline in Pediatric Septic Shock: A GRADE-Assessed Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials.

J Intensive Care Med · Elbahloul MA et al. · DOI

Systematic review / MAMulticenterResuscitation & critical care
Clinical 7/10 Research 5/10
Why it matters
Fluid choice during early resuscitation of children with septic shock is a common ED and critical care decision.
Design
Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials comparing balanced salt solution with normal saline in children with septic shock or sepsis.
Key findings
Six RCTs including 9743 participants found no significant mortality difference (3.5% vs 3.7%; RR 0.97, 95% CI 0.81-1.16). Balanced solutions reduced renal replacement therapy (RR 0.68, 95% CI 0.47-0.97) and hyperchloremia (RR 0.65, 95% CI 0.57-0.75).
Bottom line
Balanced solutions offer clinical advantages without a mortality benefit, supporting their use while acknowledging the need for further pediatric-specific evidence.
Why these scores

Clinical: Supports a fluid-choice decision in pediatric septic shock but does not change mortality practice.

Research: Standard systematic review methodology applied to an updated clinical question, with registered protocol and GRADE certainty assessment.

Withdrawal of Life-Sustaining Treatment and the Severity-Matched Mortality Difference Between Abusive and Unintentional Pediatric Traumatic Brain Injury: A Propensity-Matched Cohort Study of the ACS TQIP.

J Pediatr Surg · Mina AS et al. · DOI

Retrospective cohortMulticenterTrauma & injuryChild protection
Clinical 5/10 Research 7/10
Why it matters
Clarifies whether abusive head trauma is intrinsically more lethal or whether withdrawal of care explains the mortality gap, which is important for prognostication and equitable care.
Design
Retrospective ACS TQIP registry study matching 395 abusive severe TBI cases to unintentional controls on demographics, presenting severity, and hospital factors, with multiple sensitivity analyses.
Key findings
Crude mortality was 40.5% for abusive versus 17.2% for unintentional injury. In matched pairs, abusive injury had higher mortality (OR 1.39) and more withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment. One-third of the excess mortality was mediated by withdrawal of care, and the excess was confined to deaths after hospital day 1.
Bottom line
At comparable presenting severity, abusive TBI has a modest mortality excess that is not fully explained by withdrawal of care, reinforcing the need for standardized neurocritical care and abuse evaluation.

Methods: ACS TQIP registry retrospective cohort with propensity matching and mediation decomposition.

Why these scores

Clinical: Propensity-matched comparison provides nuanced prognostic information about abusive TBI and withdrawal-of-care patterns that can inform family discussions and trauma care.

Research: Strong retrospective design with propensity matching, multiple imputation, and counterfactual decomposition to separate injury severity from care decisions.
