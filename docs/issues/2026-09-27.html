Prospective cohortMulticenterResuscitation & critical care
Clinical 7/10 Research 7/10
Why it matters

Design

Ann Emerg Med · Foster AA et al. · DOI

Prospective cohortMulticenterMental & behavioral health
Clinical 6/10 Research 6/10
Why it matters
Identifying which youth with positive suicide risk screens are at highest risk for subsequent attempts can guide ED discharge planning and follow-up.
Design
Secondary analysis of a prospective cohort of adolescents aged 12–17 years presenting to EDs from 2015–2019.
Key findings
Among 2,085 youth with positive screens, 11.6% attempted suicide and 19.9% had a mental health–related ED revisit or hospitalization within 3 months. Bottom line
These clinical factors can help stratify risk after a positive screen and inform next steps in care.

Methods: Secondary analysis of prospective cohort (PECARN ED Screen for Teens at Risk for Suicide, 2015–2019)

Why these scores

Clinical: Provides risk factors after a positive ED suicide screen that can inform disposition and follow-up planning.

Research: Multicenter prospective secondary analysis using standard regression with a clinically relevant new question.

Decreasing Ambulatory Antibiotic Treatment Duration for Acute Otitis Media and Community-Acquired Pneumonia.

Acad Pediatr · Pifko EL et al. · DOI

Quality improvementMulticenterInfection & feverED operations & quality
Clinical 7/10 Research 5/10
Why it matters
This study shows that a large-scale quality improvement effort can safely shorten antibiotic courses for common childhood infections, reducing unnecessary exposure and antibiotic days without increasing treatment failure.
Design
A multicenter quality improvement initiative using plan-do-study-act cycles, electronic health record tools, education, and data feedback, with outcomes tracked via statistical process control charts.
Key findings
Among 51,056 patients, short-duration antibiotic therapy rose from 40% to 88%, with gains across all race, ethnicity, and child opportunity groups. Mean antibiotic duration fell from 8 to 5.9 days, saving 42,141 cumulative antibiotic days. Treatment failure stayed stable at 5% (range 2.5%–7.8%).
Bottom line
System-wide practice change to shorter antibiotic courses for acute otitis media and community-acquired pneumonia is feasible and safe, with no increase in treatment failure.

Methods: Multisite EHR-based quality improvement study with interrupted time series analysis (statistical process control).

Why these scores

Clinical: Large multicenter QI initiative showing a system-wide increase in short-course antibiotics across ED, urgent care, and primary care without increased treatment failure.

Research: Uses statistical process control across a large multistate dataset, though the QI design limits causal inference.

Summary and scores are AI-generated from the abstract only. Check the original article before changing practice.

CT Use for Pediatric Mild Brain Injuries by Hospital Type: 2017 to 2023.

Pediatrics · Paik AJ et al. · DOI

Retrospective cohortMulticenterTrauma & injuryImagingED operations & quality
Clinical 5/10 Research 4/10
Why it matters
This study shows that head CT use in children with mild brain injuries remains high despite guidelines, and that rates differ meaningfully by hospital type, suggesting where quality improvement efforts may be needed.
Design
Retrospective analysis of U.S. trauma registry data from 2017 to 2023, comparing annual head CT rates across adult, pediatric, and mixed hospitals.
Key findings
Among 12,030 children, 32.1% were treated at adult hospitals, 31.6% at pediatric hospitals, and 36.2% at mixed hospitals. Overall CT rates ranged from 43.5% to 51.5%. Adult hospitals had the highest rate at 59.5%, followed by mixed hospitals at 44.0% and pediatric hospitals at 38.8%. Higher use was seen with lower GCS, impaired consciousness, and nonteaching or smaller hospitals.
Bottom line
Head CT use in pediatric mild TBI remained high across all hospital types, indicating persistent gaps in guideline adoption and a need for targeted interventions to reduce unnecessary imaging.

Methods: National Trauma Data Bank retrospective cohort study

Why these scores

Clinical: Documents persistent high CT rates and hospital-type variation, supporting quality improvement but not changing immediate practice.

Research: Large national retrospective description with useful subgroup analyses, but standard methods and largely confirmatory findings.

Comparative Diagnostic Performance of Six Clinical Scoring Systems for Identifying Complicated Appendicitis in School-Aged Children: A Multicenter Prospective Study.

Afr J Paediatr Surg · Alyhari Q et al. · DOI

Diagnostic accuracyMulticenterSurgical abdomen & GU
Clinical 7/10 Research 7/10
Why it matters
Early identification of complicated appendicitis may guide surgical timing and resource use; comparing practical scores helps clinicians choose an effective tool.
Design
Multicenter prospective observational study of 360 children aged 10–15 years with histopathologically confirmed appendicitis. Six scoring systems were calculated before surgery and tested against the World Society of Emergency Surgery criteria for complicated disease.
Key findings
The AIR score had the highest discrimination (AUC 0.78), with sensitivity 76.8% and specificity 73.8% at a cutoff of 9 or more. It also showed the best calibration and highest net benefit on decision curve analysis. Other scores had lower AUCs ranging from 0.65 to 0.72.
Bottom line
The AIR score appears superior for ED risk stratification of complicated appendicitis in school-aged children, although external validation in broader populations is needed.

Methods: Multicenter prospective study with head-to-head AUC comparison, calibration analysis, and decision curve analysis.

Why these scores

Clinical: Head-to-head comparison of six bedside scores supports selecting AIR score for ED risk stratification of complicated appendicitis.

Research: Multicenter prospective comparison with AUC, calibration, and decision curve analysis offers a transferable evaluative framework.

Retrospective cohortAI / LLMMental & behavioral healthED operations & quality
Clinical 4/10 Research 7/10
Why it matters
Pediatric psychiatric ED visits are rising, yet psychotropic prescribing is poorly characterized; identifying drivers of in-ED use versus discharge prescriptions can inform safer care.
Design
Retrospective cohort of 1,527 patients (2,060 psychiatric ED visits) at a French children's hospital using a clinical data warehouse; logistic regression and gradient boosting integrated structured data and SNOMED concepts from free text.

Psychotropics were administered in 21.9% of visits and prescribed at discharge in 13.9%. Methods: Single-center clinical data warehouse cohort with machine learning and NLP of free-text notes.

A cluster analysis of neonates using clinical signs of possible serious bacterial infection at hospital admission in Kenya: A retrospective multicentre cohort study.

PLoS One · Tuti T et al. · DOI

Retrospective cohortMulticenterAI / LLMInfection & fever
Clinical 6/10 Research 7/10
Why it matters
Neonatal sepsis is a leading cause of death in sub-Saharan Africa, and current management is uniform. A simple, admission-based risk stratification tool could help target care and resources.
Design
Retrospective multicentre cohort study using latent class analysis on signs of possible serious bacterial infection at admission, with external validation in separate hospitals.
Key findings
Five clusters with increasing mortality risk were identified. The model discriminated in-hospital mortality with AUROC 0.867, outperforming the WHO sepsis severity classification (AUROC 0.721), with good calibration.
Bottom line
This clustering approach may complement clinician judgment for neonatal sepsis risk at admission, but prospective studies are needed before clinical use.

Methods: Retrospective multicentre cohort of 33,094 neonates from 8 Kenyan hospitals with external validation in 23,704 neonates from 13 hospitals; latent class analysis.

Why these scores

Clinical: Provides a new risk-stratification tool for neonatal sepsis at admission, but requires prospective validation before changing practice.

Research: Applies latent class analysis to admission signs in a large multicentre African neonatal cohort with external validation and comparison to WHO classification.
