Casting Without Reduction Versus Reduction With or Without Fixation for Mildly Displaced Pediatric Distal Radius Metaphyseal Fractures: A Randomized Noninferiority Trial.

J Pediatr Orthop · García-Rueda MF et al. · DOI

RCTTrauma & injury
Clinical 8/10 Research 5/10
Why it matters
This trial challenges the routine practice of reducing mildly displaced distal radius fractures under general anesthesia in children, potentially reducing risks and costs.
Design
Randomized, open-label, noninferiority trial at a single tertiary pediatric orthopaedic hospital; 60 children aged 5-10 years with mildly displaced distal radius metaphyseal fractures were randomized to immobilization without reduction or closed reduction under anesthesia.
Key findings
Mean PROMIS upper-extremity scores at 6 months were nearly identical (45.59 vs 45.62; difference -0.03, 95% CI -4.71 to 4.65), meeting the noninferiority margin. No serious adverse events in the no-reduction group; two anesthetic complications in the reduction group.
Bottom line
For children with isolated distal radius metaphyseal fractures with shortening ≤10 mm and/or angulation 10-20 degrees, casting without reduction is a safe and effective alternative to reduction under anesthesia.
Why these scores

Clinical: Noninferiority trial showing casting without reduction is noninferior to reduction under anesthesia, potentially avoiding unnecessary procedures.

Research: Single-center open-label RCT with small sample; noninferiority design is standard but question is clinically important.

Low-Level Pyuria and Antibiotic Prescribing for Suspected Urinary Tract Infection in Children.

Pediatrics · Wattier RL et al. · DOI

Cross-sectionalMulticenterInfection & fever
Clinical 6/10 Research 5/10
Why it matters
Low-level pyuria may drive unnecessary antibiotics for suspected UTI in children.
Design
Cross-sectional EHR/symptom study of ED and urgent care visits at four Northern California sites in 2022-2023.
Key findings
Among 1,150 children (median 6.3 years), 191 (16.6%) received antibiotics. Low-level pyuria (trace leukocyte esterase, 10-69 WBC/mm3, 5-10 WBC/HPF) was associated with empiric antibiotic initiation (aOR 6.62, 95% CI 3.72-11.79) vs no pyuria. It accounted for 17.0% (95% CI 10.3%-23.2%) of overtreatment and 1.8% (95% CI 1.1%-2.4%) of confirmed UTI treatment; age strata were similar.
Bottom line
Interpret low pyuria in clinical context; systems should reduce its excess influence to avoid overtreatment.

Methods: Electronic health record and symptom data; cross-sectional study from 4 Northern California ED/urgent care sites.

Why these scores

Clinical: Cross-sectional evidence that low pyuria prompts antibiotics and contributes to overtreatment; supports more nuanced UTI decision-making.

Research: Standard multivariable regression plus attributable-fraction analysis in a multi-site pediatric ED/urgent care dataset; useful for stewardship interventions.

Balanced Crystalloids Versus Normal Saline for Fluid Resuscitation in Pediatric Septic Shock: An Updated Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

J Clin Med Res · Alshahrani HS et al. · DOI

Systematic review / MAResuscitation & critical care
Clinical 8/10 Research 8/10
Why it matters
Fluid choice in pediatric septic shock is a common ED decision; this meta-analysis including PRoMPT BOLUS shows balanced crystalloids reduce hyperchloremia and RRT need, though mortality and AKI remain inconclusive.
Design
Systematic review and meta-analysis of 9 studies (5 RCTs, 4 cohorts) with trial sequential analysis.
Key findings
No significant difference in mortality (RR 0.94) or AKI (RR 0.84), but significant reduction in RRT (RR 0.74) and hyperchloremia (RR 0.65).
Bottom line
Balanced crystalloids are a reasonable choice, but evidence for hard outcomes is insufficient; further trials needed.

Methods: Systematic review with trial sequential analysis of RCTs and cohorts.

Why these scores

Clinical: Updated meta-analysis informs fluid choice in septic shock, showing benefits on secondary outcomes but inconclusive mortality.

Research: Trial sequential analysis and inclusion of recent PRoMPT BOLUS trial add methodological strength.

Predictors of Antibiotic Overuse in Febrile Children: A Sub-analysis of the Prospective, Multicenter AutoPilot-Dx Study.

Pediatr Infect Dis J · Papan C et al. · DOI

Prospective cohortMulticenterInfection & feverED operations & quality
Clinical 6/10 Research 6/10
Why it matters
Antibiotic overuse in febrile children fuels resistance, and understanding its drivers can guide stewardship.
Design
Sub-analysis of a prospective multicenter study of febrile children (90 days–18 years) with respiratory infection or fever without source, using multivariable logistic regression.
Key findings
Among 628 viral-confirmed cases, 186 (29.6%) had antibiotic overuse. Independent predictors were diagnostic uncertainty (OR 2.09, 95% CI 1.18–3.93), nonexudative tonsillitis (OR 2.78, 95% CI 1.72–4.59), and rales (OR 1.82, 95% CI 1.15–2.86). Younger age was associated in complete-case analysis (OR 0.93, 95% CI 0.85–0.99) but not after multiple imputation (OR 0.96, 95% CI 0.91–1.01).
Bottom line
Diagnostic uncertainty and specific clinical findings drive antibiotic overuse; better diagnostic tools may reduce unnecessary prescribing.

Methods: Prospective multicenter cohort (AutoPilot-Dx) sub-analysis with logistic regression

Why these scores

Clinical: Identifies diagnostic uncertainty and clinical findings as predictors of antibiotic overuse in febrile children in the ED.

Research: Prospective multicenter sub-analysis with sensitivity analysis; standard methods but valuable data.

Pediatric Recurrent Croup: An International Delphi Consensus Statement.

Laryngoscope · Crotty TJ et al. · DOI

GuidelineMulticenterRespiratory
Clinical 6/10 Research 6/10
Why it matters
Recurrent croup varies widely in definition and workup, so clinicians lack a standard approach.
Design
International modified Delphi consensus; literature review of 28 studies plus two rating rounds on a 9-point scale.
Key findings
Strong agreement favored direct laryngoscopy/bronchoscopy for atypical cases, including subglottic sizing and laryngeal cleft exclusion; allergy testing only with atopic suspicion. Consensus supported shared ENT-respiratory care, avoiding routine imaging, and reserving advanced reflux testing for refractory cases. No consensus on empirical inhaled corticosteroids or antireflux therapy.
Bottom line
A risk-stratified algorithm may standardize evaluation, but medical management remains uncertain and needs prospective study.

Methods: Literature review (PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Cochrane, EMBASE) + international modified Delphi consensus.

Why these scores

Clinical: Consensus provides guidance for ED evaluation of recurrent croup, but based on limited evidence.

Research: First international consensus on recurrent croup, but standard Delphi method with low-quality evidence base.
