The Epidemiology of Paediatric Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest in Australia and New Zealand: A Report from The Australasian Resuscitation Outcomes Consortium Epistry (Aus-ROC).

Resuscitation · Saha A et al. · DOI

Prospective cohortMulticenterResuscitation & critical carePrehospital & EMS
Clinical 6/10 Research 6/10
Why it matters
This is the first large study describing pediatric out-of-hospital cardiac arrest across Australia and New Zealand, showing that incidence, arrest features, and survival differ strongly by age and may need age-specific emergency care planning.
Design
Observational analysis of EMS-attended cardiac arrests in children under 18 years from 2017 to 2023, using registry data and logistic regression to examine predictors of survival.
Key findings
EMS attended 4,779 cases and treated 3,139 (65.7%). Incidence was 10.1 per 100,000 person-years for attended and 6.6 for treated cases, with peaks in infants (65.4) and adolescents (11.4). Most arrests were unwitnessed (66.9%), medical (60.1%), non-shockable (91.1%), and at home (74.3%). Bystander CPR occurred in 62.7%, but defibrillation in only 1.6%. Survival to 30 days/discharge among treated cases was 10.0%, highest in late childhood (15.4%) and lowest in infants (7.9%).
Bottom line
Age-specific prevention and resuscitation strategies are needed because pediatric arrest patterns and outcomes vary substantially across developmental stages.

Methods: Multinational EMS registry cohort (Aus-ROC Epistry) with multivariable logistic regression.

Why these scores

Clinical: Large EMS registry describes pOHCA epidemiology and age-specific outcomes but does not directly change bedside management.

Research: Standard registry epidemiology, but this is the first comprehensive Australasian pOHCA report with age-stratified survival analysis and clear follow-up questions.

#2

Federal Immigration Enforcement Deployment and Emergency Department Utilization Among Children.

JAMA Netw Open · Rolin AI et al. · DOI

Other designED operations & qualityEquity & access
Clinical 5/10 Research 6/10
Why it matters
This study shows that a sudden federal immigration enforcement operation was linked to fewer children coming to a pediatric emergency department, especially Spanish-speaking and Hispanic families, suggesting enforcement activity may deter families from seeking emergency care.
Design
Repeated cross-sectional interrupted time series analysis comparing daily pediatric ED visits in 2025 before and after federal deployment with the same calendar period in 2024 at one children's hospital in Washington, DC.
Key findings
Among 21,822 visits, Spanish-preferring patients had an immediate drop of 10.4 visits per day after deployment versus an increase of 5.6 among English-preferring patients (difference -16.0). Hispanic patients dropped by 13.3 visits per day versus a rise of 6.2 among non-Hispanic patients (difference -19.4). No comparable declines occurred in 2024.
Bottom line
Federal immigration enforcement deployment was associated with an immediate reduction in pediatric ED use, particularly among Spanish-speaking and Hispanic children, pointing to unintended consequences for children's emergency care access.

Methods: Single-center repeated cross-sectional interrupted time series (2025 vs 2024).

Why these scores

Clinical: Background ED utilization trend with equity implications; unlikely to change individual clinical decisions but informs ED operations and outreach.

Research: Natural experiment using interrupted time series with language and ethnicity stratification; standard method applied to a novel policy exposure in pediatric ED care.

Optimizing Antibiotic Duration for Pneumonia and Sinusitis in the Pediatric Emergency Department.

Pediatrics · Saucedo JA et al. · DOI

Quality improvementED operations & qualityInfection & fever
Clinical 5/10 Research 4/10
Why it matters
Shorter antibiotic courses for uncomplicated pneumonia and sinusitis can reduce side effects and resistance, but emergency prescribers often continue longer durations. This project shows that targeted quality improvement efforts can change prescribing practice in a pediatric emergency setting.
Design
Single-center quality improvement initiative using clinical pathway changes, electronic health record updates, prescriber education, and iterative Plan-Do-Study-Act cycles.
Key findings
The percentage of children receiving guideline-recommended antibiotic durations increased from 13.1% to 64.8%, with three distinct improvement shifts. Use of pathway and order-set tools rose over time, and the 14-day return visit rate for the same diagnosis did not change.
Bottom line
Combining updated pathways, electronic health record tools, education, and feedback safely and substantially improved antibiotic duration prescribing for pneumonia and sinusitis.

Methods: Single-center quality improvement report with Plan-Do-Study-Act cycles.

Why these scores

Clinical: Supports ED antibiotic stewardship decisions by showing a QI bundle can increase guideline-concordant short antibiotic durations without increasing return visits.

Research: Standard PDSA quality improvement methodology applied to a known antibiotic stewardship question in a pediatric ED, with balancing metric but no comparison group.

Development and independent regional validation of a dynamic ultrasound appendicitis score for suspected acute appendicitis: a prospective multicenter study.

Emerg Radiol · Mohaibes M et al. · DOI

Diagnostic accuracyMulticenterSurgical abdomen & GUImaging
Clinical 7/10 Research 8/10
Why it matters
Appendicitis is the most common surgical emergency, and CT overuse in children remains a concern. A validated ultrasound-based score with interactions could improve risk stratification in the ED.
Design
Prospective multicenter diagnostic accuracy study with development (n=802) and independent regional external validation (n=262) cohorts.
Key findings
The Dynamic UAS showed excellent discrimination (AUC 0.96 development, 0.94 validation), outperforming existing scores. At a cutoff of 6, sensitivity was 91.7%/90.7% and specificity 97.1%/96.0%. In non-visualized appendix cases, AUC was 0.89 in external validation.
Bottom line
This independently validated web-based score may reduce unnecessary CT in children with suspected appendicitis, though further international validation is needed.

Methods: Prospective multicenter derivation and external validation cohort with multivariable logistic regression and interaction terms.

Why these scores

Clinical: A validated dynamic ultrasound score could reduce unnecessary CT in children with suspected appendicitis, supporting ED imaging decisions.

Research: Prospective multicenter derivation with interaction terms, external regional validation, bootstrapping, and sensitivity analyses for an understudied pediatric-relevant diagnostic question.

#5

Azithromycin for acute wheezing and bronchiolitis in children younger than six years: A systematic review and meta-analysis.

Paediatr Respir Rev · da Silva Menegazzo Miotto DA et al. · DOI

Systematic review / MARespiratory
Clinical 7/10 Research 6/10
Why it matters
Off-label azithromycin is sometimes prescribed for preschool wheezing and bronchiolitis despite guideline warnings; this synthesis clarifies whether it works.
Design
Systematic review and meta-analysis of 10 double-blind placebo-controlled randomized trials including 2,071 children younger than six years.
Key findings
No effect on the acute episode overall or in emergency department and ward patients, and no reduction in hospitalization or recurrent wheeze. Apparent benefit was confined to intensive-care and home-treated recurrent episodes.
Bottom line
Azithromycin should not be used routinely for acute wheezing or bronchiolitis in young children in ED and ward settings.
Why these scores

Clinical: High-quality synthesis confirming no benefit of azithromycin for acute wheezing/bronchiolitis, reinforcing existing guidance against routine use.

Research: Standard meta-analytic methods applied to a clinically important question with subgroup analysis by care setting, but otherwise confirmatory.

Benchmarking Gemini 1.5 Pro in Pediatric Emergency Radiology: Sensitivity Gap and Physiological Bias in the Immature Skeleton.

Diagnostics (Basel) · Benelli C et al. · DOI

Diagnostic accuracyAI / LLMTrauma & injuryImaging
Clinical 6/10 Research 7/10
Why it matters
Evaluates whether a readily available general-purpose AI model could help non-specialists interpret pediatric fracture radiographs in acute settings.
Design
Retrospective diagnostic accuracy study of 182 pediatric radiographs (112 fractures, 70 controls) using Gemini 1.5 Pro with a four-question prompt.
Key findings
Sensitivity was 90.2% and specificity 87.1%; sensitivity was 100% for displaced fractures but 84.5% for non-displaced ones; growth plates caused a 12.8% false-positive rate; management matched international standards in 95% of correctly identified fractures.
Bottom line
The model is promising for obvious trauma but not safe alone in children because of growth-plate errors; expert review remains necessary.

Methods: Single-center retrospective radiograph dataset tested with a commercial large multimodal model (Gemini 1.5 Pro).

Clinical: Supports fracture diagnosis decisions in acute pediatric care but requires expert oversight because of growth-plate misinterpretation.

Research: Applies a commercial multimodal LLM zero-shot to pediatric fracture radiographs, a novel approach in PEM, though limited by single-center retrospective design.

Prehospital Risk Stratification for Early Transfusion in Pediatric Trauma: A National Trauma Data Bank Analysis.

Prehosp Emerg Care · Su J et al. · DOI

Retrospective cohortMulticenterAI / LLMPrehospital & EMSTrauma & injuryResuscitation & critical care
Clinical 6/10 Research 6/10
Why it matters
Pediatric trauma patients who need early blood transfusion are hard to identify in the prehospital setting, and validated criteria are limited. This study tests whether simple signs available to EMS crews can flag high-risk children before arrival.
Design
This was a retrospective cohort study using National Trauma Data Bank records from 2017–2020. It included 79,764 children aged 1–15 transported by EMS directly from the scene. Five prehospital predictors were tested against transfusion within 4 hours of emergency department arrival.
Key findings
Early transfusion occurred in 3.3% of children. GCS ≤14 was the strongest predictor (adjusted odds ratio 8.38), followed by penetrating mechanism (5.87) and age-adjusted hypotension (5.39). A high-risk tier containing 3.3% of patients had a 36.7% transfusion rate and captured 36.8% of all early transfusions, with 97.8% specificity and negative predictive value but only 36.8% sensitivity.
Bottom line
Five EMS-observable features identify a small group of children at very high risk of early transfusion. However, the low sensitivity means these criteria cannot safely rule out the need for transfusion on their own.
Why these scores

Clinical: A parsimonious EMS-based prediction tool could support prehospital transfusion decisions but needs prospective validation.

Research: Standard registry-derived prediction modeling with internal validation and sensitivity analyses in a large pediatric trauma cohort.

Multidomain Post-Concussive Symptom Trajectories and Biopsychosocial Correlates Following Pediatric Concussion.

J Neurotrauma · Geraghty DAP et al. · DOI

Prospective cohortTrauma & injuryNeurology
Clinical 5/10 Research 6/10
Why it matters
Understanding how concussion symptoms change across different domains can help emergency clinicians identify children who may need closer follow-up and psychosocial support after a head injury.
Design
This was a prospective longitudinal study of 326 children aged 5–18 years seen in a pediatric emergency department after concussion, with symptom assessments repeated over 3 months.
Key findings
Five recovery patterns were identified: resilient (14%), early recovery (9%), typical recovery (45%), delayed symptom exacerbation (7%), and persistently high symptoms (25%). Children with persisting symptoms were more likely to have prior mental health history and post-injury psychosocial difficulties than injury-related risk factors.
Bottom line
Recovery is not uniform; early screening for psychosocial and mental health factors may help identify children at risk for prolonged post-concussive symptoms.

Methods: Single-center prospective cohort with group-based multi-trajectory modeling

Why these scores

Clinical: Describes recovery trajectories after pediatric concussion and supports early psychosocial risk stratification, but does not directly change acute ED management.

Research: Multi-trajectory modeling across symptom domains is applied thoughtfully to longitudinal pediatric concussion data, but the single-center design limits inference.
