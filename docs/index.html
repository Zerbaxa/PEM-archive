Weight Estimation and Dose Calculation in Resuscitation: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

Pediatrics · Tijssen JA et al. · DOI

Systematic review / MAResuscitation & critical care
Clinical 7/10 Research 6/10
Why it matters
Accurate weight estimation is critical for drug dosing in pediatric resuscitation; this meta-analysis suggests a better tool.
Design
Systematic review and meta-analysis of 250 studies, 9 involving children requiring resuscitation.
Key findings
PAWPER tape was superior to Broselow for time to drug administration, dose accuracy, and weight estimation (OR 2.48 for P10). Age-based formulas were inaccurate.
Bottom line

Why these scores

Research: Standard meta-analysis but addresses a specific gap in resuscitation drug dosing.

Violent Reinjury Trajectories Across Injury Mechanisms: A Multicenter Cohort Study.

Ann Emerg Med · Szoko N et al. · DOI

Retrospective cohortMulticenterTrauma & injuryEquity & access
Clinical 5/10 Research 5/10
Why it matters
This large multicenter study identifies which survivors of violent injury are most likely to be reinjured, and how the type of reinjury may escalate. The findings can help target prevention programs toward high-risk groups, such as younger firearm-injured patients and those in socially vulnerable areas.
Design
Observational cohort study across four Level I trauma centers (two adult, two pediatric) from 2016 to 2023, using multivariable Cox proportional hazards models to assess time to violent reinjury.
Key findings
Among 29,038 nonfatal violent injury survivors, male sex, Black/other race, adult hospital presentation, public insurance, and high social vulnerability were each associated with greater risk of any reinjury. For firearm index injuries, patients under 18 had higher risk of firearm reinjury than those 18–26 (aHR 0.65) and those over 26 (aHR 0.47). Women with firearm injury had lower firearm reinjury risk (aHR 0.46) but higher blunt reinjury risk (aHR 1.77). High social vulnerability was linked to escalation from blunt to firearm (aHR 1.46).
Bottom line
Demographic and social factors strongly shape violent reinjury risk and mechanism, suggesting that prevention efforts should be tailored to specific subgroups, especially younger firearm-injured patients and those in socially vulnerable communities.

Methods: Multicenter cohort study (4 trauma centers) with Cox proportional hazards modeling.

Why these scores

Clinical: Identifies risk factors for violent reinjury but does not directly change ED management.

Research: Multicenter cohort with standard Cox models; novel focus on reinjury trajectories across mechanisms.

Retrospective cohortMulticenterTrauma & injuryED operations & quality
Clinical 7/10 Research 8/10
Why it matters

Predicting survival in pediatric out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: Development and internal validation of risk scores.

Am J Emerg Med · Mawani M et al. · DOI

Retrospective cohortMulticenterResuscitation & critical carePrehospital & EMS
Clinical 6/10 Research 6/10
Why it matters
This large registry study offers simple, prehospital-based risk scores that can help categorize children after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest into low, moderate, or high chance of survival to discharge, which may support research, quality monitoring, and eventual clinical guidance.
Design
Retrospective analysis of a U.S. cardiac arrest registry from 2013 to 2023, using 70% of data to develop risk scores and 30% for internal validation; logistic regression and classification trees were compared.
Key findings
Among 26,895 pediatric patients, 27.8% survived to admission and 11.9% to discharge. Separate scores were made for infants, preschool/school-age children, and adolescents. Key predictors included gender, arrest location, witness status, cause, bystander CPR, prehospital AED use, and first rhythm. Discrimination was good, with area under the ROC curves around 0.76–0.82. Average survival by risk group ranged from 3–4% in low-risk, 11–13% in moderate-risk, and 29–48% in high-risk groups.
Bottom line
Pediatric OHCA survival can be stratified into three meaningful risk categories using a small set of easily identified prehospital variables, with tree-based models offering simpler use and similar accuracy.

Methods: U.S. cardiac arrest registry cohort + derivation/validation risk score study

Why these scores

Clinical: Risk scores using prehospital variables may support prognostication and quality monitoring, but require external validation.

Research: Large registry-based development of risk scores with internal validation only; CART and LR comparison is standard.

Diagnostic accuracyInfection & feverED operations & quality
Clinical 7/10 Research 5/10
Why it matters

Design
Retrospective external validation of the Sainte-Justine four-predictor rule in 130 children at a Swiss tertiary PED from 2015 to 2025.
Key findings
Sensitivity was 97.4% and specificity 69.2%; NPV was 94.7%. Both false-negatives were MSSA bacteremia without indwelling devices. Among contaminants, 71% received antibiotics under usual care versus 31% classified as moderate or high risk.

Research: Standard external validation of a derived rule in an independent single-center cohort; multicenter validation is the clear next step.

Electrical Cardiometry for Non-Invasive Cardiac Output Monitoring in Neonatal and Pediatric Critical Care: A Bayesian Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

J Intensive Care Med · Fernández-Sarmiento J et al. · DOI

Systematic review / MAResuscitation & critical careCardiology
Clinical 4/10 Research 7/10
Why it matters
Noninvasive cardiac output monitoring could guide resuscitation and hemodynamic management in children in emergency, ICU, and transport settings without invasive catheters.
Design
Systematic review and Bayesian hierarchical random-effects meta-analysis of 6 studies including 448 neonates and children, searched through March 2026.
Key findings
Pooled posterior mean percentage error was 26.9% (95% CrI 24.3-29.5%), with a 98.3% posterior probability that the true percentage error remained within 30%. Stroke volume bias was 2.82 mL, and a pilot randomized trial suggested monitoring-guided management reduced fluid administration and accelerated vasoactive therapy initiation.
Bottom line
Electrical cardiometry appears clinically acceptable as a noninvasive cardiac output monitor in children, but confirmation in larger prospective multicenter studies is needed before routine use.

Methods: Systematic review with Bayesian hierarchical meta-analysis of method-comparison and outcome studies.

Why these scores

Clinical: Supports a monitoring technology but does not yet provide a definitive emergency care protocol; larger multicenter validation is still needed.

Research: Bayesian hierarchical meta-analysis is uncommon in pediatric emergency/critical care monitoring research, though the evidence base is small and heterogeneous.

Complications in neonates with difficult tracheal intubation: an analysis of the Pediatric Difficult Intubation Registry.

Anaesthesia · Stein ML et al. · DOI

Retrospective cohortMulticenterResuscitation & critical care
Clinical 4/10 Research 6/10
Why it matters
Difficult intubation in newborns is particularly dangerous, and this large registry comparison quantifies how much higher their complication burden is than older children, supporting focused efforts to improve airway management in this age group.
Design
Retrospective analysis of the Pediatric Difficult Intubation Registry comparing outcomes between neonates and older children, with logistic regression for factors linked to neonatal complications.
Key findings
Among 6283 difficult intubations, 420 were neonates. First-attempt success was lower (33.1% vs 51.1%) and complications more common (29.3% vs 13.6%) in neonates than older children. Hypoxia occurred in 21.9% vs 5.7%; severe complications in 2.9% vs 0.6%; cardiac arrest in 1.0% vs 0.2%; difficult mask ventilation in 14.3% vs 7.5%. More intubation attempts (aOR 1.61 per attempt) and difficult facemask ventilation (aOR 3.17) increased complications; anticipated difficulty was protective (aOR 0.37).
Bottom line
In neonates with difficult intubation, risk rises with repeated attempts and difficult mask ventilation; anticipating problems and planning to minimize attempts may reduce harm.

Methods: Pediatric Difficult Intubation Registry; retrospective cohort analysis with multivariable logistic regression

Why these scores

Clinical: Supports safer neonatal intubation strategies by quantifying risk factors like multiple attempts and difficult mask ventilation, but does not directly change a specific ED decision.

Research: Large multicenter registry analysis with adjusted outcomes, filling a specific gap on neonatal difficult intubation, though methods are standard for registry studies.

Measurement-Informed and Fluid-Sparing Cardiovascular Resuscitation Strategies in Pediatric Septic Shock: A Systematic Review and Exploratory Meta-Analysis.

J Intensive Care Med · Cunha LAD et al. · DOI

Systematic review / MAResuscitation & critical careInfection & fever
Clinical 5/10 Research 6/10
Why it matters
Pediatric septic shock resuscitation needs to balance tissue perfusion with avoiding excess fluid, and this review highlights that different resuscitation approaches exist with unclear overall benefit.
Design
Prospectively registered systematic review and exploratory meta-analysis of comparative studies in pediatric septic shock.
Key findings
Six studies including 422 children were analyzed. Mortality was not statistically different between randomized strategies (RR 0.88, 95% CI 0.60-1.28; very low certainty). Measurement-informed strategies showed a mortality RR of 0.77 (95% CI 0.46-1.27). Two small trials found lower 24-hour fluid intake with measurement-informed care (mean difference -11.47 mL/kg, 95% CI -16.88 to -6.05; moderate certainty). Acute kidney injury results were very uncertain (RR 0.48, 95% CI 0.15-1.57). Other outcomes could not be pooled statistically.
Bottom line
Current evidence does not prove that any specific strategy improves survival or other patient-centered outcomes, though measurement-informed resuscitation may reduce early fluid administration. Further trials linking physiological measurements or treatment rules to standard outcomes are needed.

Methods: Systematic review and exploratory meta-analysis of randomized and nonrandomized comparative studies.

Why these scores

Clinical: This exploratory systematic review does not establish a mortality benefit or equivalence among resuscitation strategies, so it mainly informs future research rather than changing current PED practice.

Research: A registered systematic review with risk-of-bias and GRADE assessment, but the exploratory meta-analysis is limited by small studies and heterogeneous interventions.
