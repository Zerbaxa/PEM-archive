Instrumental Support Calls vs. Caring Contacts Texts to Prevent Suicide in Adolescents and Adults: The SPARC Randomized Clinical Trial.

Am J Psychiatry · Radin AK et al. · DOI

RCTMulticenterMental & behavioral health
Clinical 9/10 Research 7/10
Why it matters
Suicide risk screening in EDs is common, but optimal post-discharge follow-up is unclear; this trial provides age-specific evidence.
Design
Pragmatic comparative effectiveness RCT across 9 EDs and 36 primary care clinics, randomizing 723 adolescents and 797 adults to instrumental support calls or caring contacts texts.
Key findings
At 6 months, adults had higher suicidal ideation/behavior scores with texts than calls; at 12 months, adolescents had lower scores with texts than calls. Outcomes were otherwise similar.
Bottom line
Post-ED suicide prevention follow-up should be tailored by age: connection-focused texts for adolescents, early instrumental calls for adults.

Methods: Pragmatic multicenter RCT with age-stratified randomization across EDs and primary care clinics.

Why these scores

Clinical: Large pragmatic RCT showing age-dependent benefit of two post-ED suicide prevention follow-up strategies, directly informing discharge planning for adolescents after ED suicide risk screening.

Research: Pragmatic multicenter RCT with age-stratified randomization comparing two follow-up interventions; robust design and clear age-specific findings.

Utility of MRI as sole cross-sectional imaging for pediatric cervical spine clearance following radiography.

J Pediatr Surg · Kahan AM et al. · DOI

Prospective cohortMulticenterTrauma & injuryImaging
Clinical 8/10 Research 6/10
Why it matters
Children with abnormal cervical spine X-rays or persistent pain often get both CT and MRI; this analysis questions CT's added value.
Design
Secondary analysis of a multicenter prospective cohort of pediatric blunt cervical spine imaging at 72 US trauma centers.
Key findings
Among 106 children with abnormal X-rays, 15.1% had CT-positive but MRI-negative findings, yet none were clinically significant or unstable. Among 71 with normal X-rays and persistent pain, 11.3% were discordant with no significant CT-only injuries; a direct MRI pathway found no clinically significant injury needing rescue CT.
Bottom line
When cross-sectional imaging is indicated, MRI alone is preferable when feasible; routine CT plus MRI is not supported. Outpatient reassessment with collar immobilization may be reasonable in selected neurologically intact children.
Why these scores

Clinical: This secondary analysis of a multicenter prospective cohort suggests MRI alone is sufficient after abnormal XR or persistent pain, potentially reducing CT use in pediatric cervical spine clearance.

Research: Leverages a large prospective multicenter trauma imaging cohort to answer a focused question about unnecessary dual cross-sectional imaging, with clear clinical follow-up.

#3

Efficacy and safety of intranasal and buccal midazolam versus intravenous, intramuscular, and rectal benzodiazepines for the termination of acute seizures in children: a systematic review and meta-analysis.

Seizure · Alsabri M et al. · DOI

Systematic review / MANeurology
Clinical 8/10 Research 6/10
Why it matters
Children with acute seizures in the ED or prehospital need rapid, safe benzodiazepine delivery; route choice affects time to control.
Design
Systematic review and meta-analysis of 23 randomized controlled trials (2238 children) comparing intranasal or buccal midazolam with intravenous, intramuscular, rectal, or oral benzodiazepines.
Key findings
Seizure cessation at 10 minutes was comparable overall (RR 1.04, 95% CI 0.99-1.09). Transmucosal midazolam was better than rectal diazepam (RR 1.18), reduced 60-minute seizure recurrence (RR 0.62), was administered faster, and shortened time to seizure control; respiratory adverse events did not differ.
Bottom line
Intranasal or buccal midazolam is an effective, safe first-line rescue option and may be preferred when IV access is not immediately available.
Why these scores

Clinical: High-quality meta-analysis of RCTs supporting transmucosal midazolam as a fast, safe first-line option for pediatric seizure termination.

Research: Comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis comparing administration routes, with standard methodology and useful subgroup comparisons.

Timing of the Adrenaline Administration in Witnessed Pediatric Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest: Retrospective, National Cohort Study in Japan, Using a 2014-2020 Dataset.

Pediatr Crit Care Med · Okajima K et al. · DOI

Retrospective cohortMulticenterResuscitation & critical carePrehospital & EMS
Clinical 6/10 Research 8/10
Why it matters
The optimal timing of epinephrine in pediatric cardiac arrest is uncertain, and observational studies are prone to resuscitation time bias.
Design
Retrospective analysis of the Japanese JAAM-OHCA registry (2014-2020) using risk-set matching with time-dependent propensity scores to compare adrenaline administration in specified time intervals after EMS contact.
Key findings
Among 259 witnessed pediatric OHCA patients, risk ratios for 1-month survival were not significantly different across time intervals (e.g., RR 2.94 for 1-10 min, 0.97 for 11-15 min, 1.00 for 16-20 min, 1.35 for 21-25 min, 1.71 for 26-30 min). No association was found between earlier adrenaline and survival or favorable neurologic outcome.
Bottom line
In this registry, earlier adrenaline administration was not associated with improved outcomes in pediatric OHCA, though wide confidence intervals limit strong conclusions.

Methods: National OHCA registry + risk-set matching with time-dependent propensity scores.

Why these scores

Clinical: Observational national study with risk-set matching; provides evidence on adrenaline timing but not definitive.

Research: Novel application of time-dependent propensity score matching to address resuscitation time bias in pediatric OHCA.

Who's on Airway? Trainee Tracheal Intubation Frequency and Outcomes Within the National Emergency Airway Registry for Pediatric Emergency Medicine (NEAR4PEM).

Ann Emerg Med · Jackson J et al. · DOI

Retrospective cohortMulticenterSedation, analgesia & proceduresEducation & workforce
Clinical 5/10 Research 5/10
Why it matters
This multicenter study clarifies how different types of trainees perform pediatric intubations in academic emergency departments, identifying which groups may need extra airway support or supervision.
Design
Multicenter retrospective observational study using data from a national pediatric emergency airway registry, January 2021 to December 2023.
Key findings
Among 1,404 intubations, 642 were primary attempts by trainees. First-attempt success was 73% overall: 75% for emergency medicine residents, 74% for pediatric emergency medicine fellows, and 58% for pediatric residents. Bottom line
Pediatric residents had worse intubation outcomes than emergency medicine residents, while emergency medicine residents and PEM fellows had comparable success and safety.

Methods: National pediatric airway registry + multicenter retrospective cohort analysis

Why these scores

Clinical: Retrospective multicenter data on trainee intubation performance supports decisions about supervision and training but does not change immediate practice.

Research: Standard registry analysis of a known question (trainee intubation outcomes) in a new comparison of trainee groups, with adjusted analyses.
