Evaluation of a Commercial Pediatric Sepsis Early Detection Alert in the Emergency Department.

JAMA Pediatr · Mitchell CM et al. · DOI

Retrospective cohortMulticenterAI / LLMInfection & feverED operations & quality
Clinical 8/10 Research 7/10
Why it matters
The Epic PESM alert is widely used in US pediatric EDs but had not been independently evaluated; this study suggests it misses most sepsis cases and may worsen inequities.
Design
Retrospective multicenter cohort of 166,943 pediatric ED encounters across 8 EDs in one New York health system, January 2024 to June 2025; sepsis defined by Phoenix criteria.
Key findings
Sensitivity was 36% (95% CI 29-43%) and PPV 3%; the alert triggered before sepsis in only 30% of cases. Sensitivity was lower in Black children (22%), children under 5 (20%), and non-tertiary EDs (25%).
Bottom line
Automated sepsis screening with this alert is not sufficient for timely or equitable detection; EDs should not rely on it alone.

Methods: Retrospective multicenter cohort across 8 EDs in one NY health system evaluating the Epic PESM alert against Phoenix sepsis criteria.

Why these scores

Clinical: Large multicenter evaluation of a widely used commercial sepsis alert; low sensitivity and disparities argue for rethinking automated sepsis screening in the ED.

Research: First independent, multicenter assessment of a widely deployed commercial sepsis alert, with subgroup analyses that expose equity gaps and generate follow-up work.

Comparative efficacy and safety of antiemetics for pediatric gastroenteritis-related vomiting in emergency departments: a network meta-analysis.

Curr Med Res Opin · Alsabri M et al. · DOI

Systematic review / MAMulticenterInfection & fever
Clinical 8/10 Research 5/10
Why it matters
Vomiting from acute gastroenteritis is a leading reason children present to the ED, but the comparative value of antiemetics was unclear.
Design
Network meta-analysis of 33 RCTs of children up to 21 years presenting to the ED with acute gastroenteritis, with ROB-2 and GRADE appraisal.
Key findings
All ondansetron formulations reduced short-term vomiting versus placebo, IV most strongly (MD -1.86, 95% CI -3.25 to -0.47), and IV ondansetron was the only agent consistently superior at intermediate follow-up. Cessation within 24 h favored IV ondansetron (RR 1.56, 95% CI 1.29-1.89) and rectal dimenhydrinate (RR 1.48, 95% CI 1.08-2.04). Ondansetron also reduced IV rehydration (RR 0.62, 95% CI 0.44-0.88) and modestly shortened ED stay.
Bottom line
Ondansetron is the most consistent first-line choice; dimenhydrinate may help longer-term control.
Why these scores

Clinical: Large network meta-analysis of RCTs on a very common ED presentation that clarifies comparative antiemetic choice and IV fluid avoidance.

Research: Standard network meta-analysis method applied to a confirmatory comparative question, but executed with ROB-2 and GRADE appraisal across 33 trials.

#3

Unfolding new horizons: Machine learning applications for pediatric intussusception - A systematic review and meta-analysis.

J Pediatr Surg · Campos LR et al. · DOI

Systematic review / MAAI / LLMSurgical abdomen & GUImaging
Clinical 6/10 Research 8/10
Why it matters
Intussusception is a common pediatric surgical emergency; diagnosis depends on operator skill, and validated ML tools could support ED triage and ultrasound interpretation by less experienced clinicians.
Design
PROSPERO-registered systematic review and meta-analysis of 11 studies (37 model variants; 36,863 patients) evaluating ML models for intussusception diagnosis or prognosis, with PROBAST and bivariate random-effects meta-analysis.
Key findings
Internally and externally validated ultrasound-based ML models showed pooled sensitivity around 0.91-0.95 and specificity around 0.96-0.98. Radiograph-based triage models performed lower (sensitivity 0.80, specificity 0.75). AI assistance improved junior readers' specificity by 15.9 percentage points and shortened examination time by 61% in one study. Prognostic AUCs varied widely.
Bottom line
ML-assisted ultrasound is promising for ED diagnosis of intussusception, but should augment rather than replace clinical ultrasound; multicenter prospective validation and clinical endpoint studies are needed before implementation.

Methods: Systematic review and meta-analysis of machine-learning diagnostic accuracy studies, with PROBAST and bivariate random-effects meta-analysis.

Why these scores

Clinical: Supports ED diagnostic decisions for intussusception by showing ML-assisted ultrasound is accurate and can help less experienced readers, though external validation and clinical endpoint data are still needed.

Research: First meta-analysis of ML diagnostic models for pediatric intussusception, with PROBAST-based risk-of-bias assessment, publication bias evaluation, and clear recommendations for prospective validation.

Abrupt versus gradual HFNC discontinuation in children: Shorter HFNC duration without increased weaning failure.

Am J Emerg Med · Mart ZÖ et al. · DOI

Prospective cohortRespiratory
Clinical 6/10 Research 5/10
Why it matters
HFNC weaning lacks evidence; prolonged use strains resources. This study compares abrupt vs gradual discontinuation in a pediatric ED.
Design
Prospective observational study in an academic pediatric ED (Sept 2025-Feb 2026). 87 children meeting improvement criteria were enrolled; physicians chose weaning strategy.
Key findings
Weaning failure occurred in 2 patients per group. Among successful weans, median HFNC duration was shorter with abrupt: 59h (IQR 44-92) vs 87h (IQR 61-110), p=0.012 (~32% reduction). LOS did not differ (p=0.065).
Bottom line
Abrupt discontinuation appears safe, reduces HFNC duration without increasing failure, and may improve device availability.

Methods: Single-center prospective observational study in an academic pediatric ED

Why these scores

Clinical: Suggests abrupt HFNC weaning is non-inferior and shorter, but needs confirmation in RCT.

Research: Prospective comparison of weaning strategies; non-randomized but clinically relevant.

Reporting of Sociodemographic and Equity-Related Factors in Emergency Department Pain Trials for Children: A Scoping Review.

Pediatr Emerg Care · Kammerer E et al. · DOI

Systematic review / MAEquity & accessSedation, analgesia & procedures
Clinical 4/10 Research 7/10
Why it matters
Equity in pediatric ED pain care depends on knowing whether trial results apply across diverse groups; this review shows current trials cannot answer that.
Design
Scoping review following JBI methodology of 65 RCTs from 2013-2025 in Canada and the USA evaluating acute pain interventions for children in EDs.
Key findings
All 65 trials reported age and 98% reported sex/gender, but only 40% reported race/ethnicity and 18% chronic illness/disability; none reported socioeconomic status or place of residence. Thirty-five trials addressed procedural pain and 30 addressed painful presentations.
Bottom line
Standardized reporting of equity-relevant demographics is needed before pediatric ED pain evidence can be considered broadly applicable.

Methods: Scoping review of 65 RCTs in pediatric ED pain using the PROGRESS-Plus framework.

Why these scores

Clinical: This is a research-reporting scoping review rather than a clinical intervention study, so it informs research standards more than immediate bedside decisions.

Research: Applies the PROGRESS-Plus equity framework to a defined body of pediatric ED pain RCTs and quantifies reporting gaps with systematic methods.

Pre-Pandemic Trends Re-emerge in Pediatric Pedestrian Injury.

Acad Pediatr · Peitzman C et al. · DOI

Retrospective cohortMulticenterTrauma & injuryEquity & access
Clinical 4/10 Research 7/10
Why it matters
Pediatric pedestrian injuries are a common ED presentation; understanding pandemic-related shifts and demographic patterns can guide injury prevention and ED resource planning.
Design
Retrospective interrupted time series of 19,958 pediatric pedestrian injury visits in the Pediatric Health Information System (2016-2023).
Key findings
Annual volumes dropped from a mean of 2,743 to 1,789 in 2020 (897 fewer than expected), then rose by 259 visits/year to 2,570 by 2023. The rebound was driven by children aged 10-15. The proportion of non-White children and daytime presentations dipped in 2020 and returned toward pre-pandemic levels by 2023.
Bottom line
The pandemic caused temporary, demographically patterned decreases in pediatric pedestrian ED visits; routine daily traffic exposure may be an important target for prevention.

Methods: Pediatric Health Information System administrative data with interrupted time series analysis.

Why these scores

Clinical: Provides epidemiological trend data on pediatric pedestrian ED visits but does not directly change acute management.

Research: Uses interrupted time series on a large multicenter pediatric ED administrative database to characterize pandemic-related changes and disparities.

The association between race, ethnicity and presentation to a pediatric capable hospital.

Acad Pediatr · James DL et al. · DOI

Cross-sectionalMulticenterED operations & qualityEquity & access
Clinical 3/10 Research 7/10
Why it matters
Pediatric readiness varies by hospital, and this study shows that race and ethnicity are associated with where children first present for emergency care, with implications for equity.
Design
Cross-sectional linkage of 2019 national ED inventory, state ED/inpatient databases, and pediatric readiness/PECC surveys, with logistic regression.
Key findings
Among over 2 million ED visits, non-Hispanic Black and Hispanic children had higher unadjusted odds of presenting to high-readiness hospitals, hospitals with a PECC, and hospitals with inpatient pediatric capability; after adjustment, Black children had higher odds of inpatient capability and Hispanic children of PECC presence.
Bottom line
PECCs and pediatric-capable hospitals may be serving a disproportionate share of minority children; ensuring readiness across all hospitals could reduce disparities.

Methods: National ED inventory and state databases linked to pediatric readiness/PECC surveys; cross-sectional logistic regression.

Why these scores

Clinical: Large administrative equity study that informs policy and ED readiness planning rather than a direct bedside decision.

Research: Novel linkage of national ED inventory data with pediatric readiness and PECC surveys to examine disparities in access to pediatric-capable EDs, with large sample and adjusted analyses.
