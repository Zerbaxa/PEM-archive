Alveolar Ventilation Efficiency Assessed via Capnography is Associated with Improved Outcomes in Pediatric Cardiac Arrest.

Am J Respir Crit Care Med · Sutton RM et al. · DOI

Prospective cohortMulticenterResuscitation & critical care
Clinical 6/10 Research 8/10
Why it matters
Airway patency during CPR may affect ventilation and outcomes; the airway opening index (AOI) had only been described in adults.
Design
Prospective multicenter observational cohort of children (<=18 years) with invasive airways and capnography data during CPR; cubic splines/ROC curves identified an AOI cutpoint evaluated with modified Poisson regression, with a sensitivity analysis excluding E-CPR.
Key findings
Among 99 events (median age 0.34 years), median AOI was 0.38. AOI >=0.35 was associated with survival to discharge (aRR 1.53, CI95 1.03-2.28) and favorable neurological outcome (aRR 1.56, CI95 1.01-2.41), and lower post-arrest peak lactates (6.0 vs. 11.5 mmol/L).
Bottom line
A capnography-derived airway patency metric is associated with pediatric CPR outcomes and merits validation as a quality target.

Methods: Prospective multicenter CPR cohort + capnography waveform-derived airway opening index

Why these scores

Clinical: First pediatric data on a capnography-derived airway patency metric during CPR, from a prospective multicenter cohort; applies to intubated in-hospital arrests.

Research: Novel capnography waveform-derived measure applied to pediatric CPR in a prospective multicenter cohort; modest size and data-derived cutpoint.

#2

Balanced crystalloids versus 0.9% saline for fluid resuscitation in children with septic shock: an updated systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials.

Ir J Med Sci · Raheem H et al. · DOI

Systematic review / MAMulticenterResuscitation & critical careInfection & fever
Clinical 8/10 Research 4/10
Why it matters
0.9% saline remains the most common bolus fluid for pediatric septic shock despite its high chloride content; fluid choice is a front-line ED resuscitation decision.
Design
PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO-registered systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis of randomized trials of balanced crystalloids versus 0.9% saline in children (< 18 years) with sepsis or septic shock.
Key findings
Six trials (10,308 children). Mortality 3.7% vs. 3.9% (RR 0.95, 95% CI 0.75-1.21); AKI not reduced (RR 0.76), renal replacement therapy reduced (RR 0.67, 0.48-0.92); hyperchloraemia (RR 0.65) and hypernatraemia (RR 0.59) reduced; hyperlactataemia more frequent (RR 1.17).
Bottom line
Balanced crystalloids are an equally safe, physiologically preferable default, but offer no mortality benefit.
Why these scores

Clinical: Updated meta-analysis of RCTs on fluid choice in pediatric septic shock, a front-line ED resuscitation decision, supporting balanced crystalloids as default.

Research: Standard PROSPERO-registered meta-analysis of RCTs updating a known question.

Summary and scores are AI-generated from the abstract only. Check the original article before changing practice.

Repeat 9-1-1 Calls and Clinical Outcomes of Children After Non-Transport by Emergency Medical Services.

Prehosp Emerg Care · Williams JEP et al. · DOI

Cross-sectionalMulticenterPrehospital & EMSED operations & quality
Clinical 6/10 Research 6/10
Why it matters
Non-transport by EMS is common in children, yet the safety of leaving a child at home after a 9-1-1 call has had little outcome data.
Design
Retrospective cross-sectional study of the 2018-2022 ESO Data Collaborative dataset (one third linked to hospital data), with multivariable logistic regression for factors associated with a repeat 9-1-1 call and transport within 72 hours.
Key findings
Of 1,849,925 pediatric 9-1-1 calls, 732,525 (40%) were not transported. Within 72 hours, 7,544 (1%) had a repeat call with transport to the ED. Repeat calls were associated with age 0-4 years, Black non-Hispanic race, male sex, very low Child Opportunity Index areas, out-of-office hours and neurologic impressions. There were 11 (0.1%) on-scene deaths at the repeat call.
Bottom line
Return after pediatric non-transport is rare; the risk factors identified can inform safer non-transport protocols.

Methods: National EMS registry with partial hospital linkage + case-control comparison

Why these scores

Clinical: Large multiagency EMS dataset suggests pediatric non-transport rarely leads to repeat call and transport, informing non-transport protocols.

Research: Standard retrospective analysis of an EMS registry with partial hospital linkage addressing a clear evidence gap.

#4

CT Use for Pediatric Mild Brain Injuries by Hospital Type: 2017 to 2023.

Pediatrics · Paik AJ et al. · DOI

Retrospective cohortMulticenterTrauma & injuryImaging
Clinical 4/10 Research 3/10
Why it matters
PECARN-based guidance has long aimed to cut head CT in children with minor head trauma, yet it is unclear how far practice has moved and where children are imaged most.
Design
National Trauma Data Bank analysis of children with isolated mild TBI, 2017-2023, comparing yearly CT rates across adult, pediatric and mixed hospitals with bootstrap confidence intervals.
Key findings
In 12,030 children, overall CT use barely changed over seven years (43.5% to 51.5%). Adult hospitals scanned most (59.5%), mixed hospitals 44.0% and pediatric hospitals 38.8%. Lower GCS, altered consciousness, and smaller or nonteaching hospitals were linked to more imaging.
Bottom line
Roughly half of children with mild TBI at trauma centers still get a head CT, with the widest gap at adult hospitals; decision-rule uptake remains a target for quality work.

Methods: National trauma registry + hospital-type comparison over 7 years

Why these scores

Clinical: Describes persistent practice variation in head CT for mild TBI by hospital type; background for imaging stewardship.

Research: Standard descriptive registry analysis of a known question.

Shall we repeat? Predicting the necessity to repeat blood testing in pediatric emergency departments of laboratory tests previously performed in the community using a machine learning model.

PLoS One · Shuchami A et al. · DOI

Retrospective cohortMulticenterAI / LLMED operations & quality
Clinical 6/10 Research 7/10
Why it matters
Children referred to the PED with recent community blood tests often have them repeated, adding cost, discomfort and delay without changing management.
Design
Retrospective analysis of community-issued CBC, electrolytes and CRP in children aged three months to 18 years, compared with repeat PED tests within 12 hours across a nationwide Health Maintenance Organization; an XGBoost model was developed to predict whether repetition was needed.
Key findings
Only 13.3%, 16.3% and 19.0% of repeated CBC, electrolyte and CRP tests were justified. The model predicted need for repetition with 91.8%, 89.1% and 79.2% accuracy; with no false negatives, accuracy was 73.9%, 67.3% and 55.4%.
Bottom line
Most repeat testing is unnecessary; an ML tool could reduce repeats of CBC and electrolytes, pending clinical evaluation.

Methods: Nationwide HMO linked community and PED laboratory data + XGBoost prediction model

Why these scores

Clinical: Addresses a common front-line PED decision (repeating recent community blood tests) with first evidence on justification rates, but model not externally validated.

Research: Novel linkage of community and PED laboratory results across a nationwide HMO with an ML decision-support model; lacks external validation.

Predictive factors for psychotropic medication prescription in child and adolescent psychiatric emergency care: a machine learning approach using a health data warehouse.

Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry · Diot T et al. · DOI

Retrospective cohortAI / LLMMental & behavioral healthED operations & quality
Clinical 4/10 Research 7/10
Why it matters
Pediatric psychiatric ED visits are rising, psychotropics are often used, and there are no clear guidelines or good descriptions of current practice.
Design
Single-center retrospective cohort of 1,527 patients (Necker Hospital, 2021-2025) using clinical data warehouse data; multilevel logistic regression and gradient boosting with structured data plus SNOMED concepts from free-text notes.
Key findings
Among 2,060 psychiatric ED visits, psychotropics were administered in 21.9% and prescribed at discharge in 13.9%. In-ED use tracked acute severity markers; discharge prescribing was linked to in-ED administration, diagnosis and socioeconomic context. Free text significantly improved prediction.
Bottom line
Acute and discharge psychotropic decisions are distinct processes; unstructured notes add information for studying them.

Methods: Single-center clinical data warehouse + NLP-derived SNOMED concepts from notes + gradient boosting and multilevel logistic regression

Why these scores

Research: Clinical data warehouse with SNOMED concepts extracted from free text plus gradient boosting, applied to a poorly described PEM practice.

Paediatric Upper Respiratory Infection in Colombia: 10-Year Nationwide Trends in Healthcare Utilization From Administrative Data (2015-2024).

J Paediatr Child Health · Buendía JA et al. · DOI

Retrospective cohortMulticenterRespiratoryED operations & quality
Clinical 3/10 Research 7/10
Why it matters
URTIs are among the commonest reasons for pediatric care, and post-pandemic shifts in where children receive care matter for ED capacity planning.
Design
Nationwide population-based time-series study using Colombia's administrative registry (RIPS) linked to population projections for ages 0-17 years, 2015-2024, with segmented Poisson models with population offsets.
Key findings
14 173 511 URTI records. Rates rose from 11 139 to 14 421 per 100 000 (2015-2019), fell to 4993 in 2020 and plateaued near 9806 by 2024, about 50% below the expected trajectory. ED and hospitalization rates remained 45% and 40% below 2019 levels. Croup/epiglottitis was 2.1% of contacts but 11.3% of URTI hospitalizations.
Bottom line
Pediatric URTI care reconfigured rather than recovered after the pandemic shock.

Methods: National administrative registry + segmented Poisson interrupted time series

Why these scores

Clinical: National utilization trends provide background for respiratory capacity planning, not bedside decisions.

Research: Nationwide administrative registry with segmented Poisson (interrupted time series) models using the pandemic as a system shock.

A cluster analysis of neonates using clinical signs of possible serious bacterial infection at hospital admission in Kenya: A retrospective multicentre cohort study.

PLoS One · Tuti T et al. · DOI

Retrospective cohortMulticenterInfection & feverResuscitation & critical care
Clinical 5/10 Research 7/10
Why it matters
Neonates with suspected sepsis in sub-Saharan Africa are managed uniformly; a risk tool using only signs available at admission could support triage where labs are scarce.
Design
Retrospective multicentre cohort of neonates <28 days admitted to 21 Kenyan hospitals (2022-2024) with >=1 pSBI sign; latent class analysis with external validation.
Key findings
Five mortality-risk clusters were derived from 33094 patients; cluster assignment reached accuracy of at least 83.16% in 23704 external patients. Mortality discrimination was AUROC 0.867 versus 0.721 for the WHO classification, with good calibration.
Bottom line
Admission-sign clusters may complement clinical judgement for neonatal sepsis risk; utility and treatment-response differences need testing.

Methods: Multicentre Kenyan hospital admission data + latent class analysis with external and internal-external validation

Why these scores

Clinical: Large externally validated admission risk stratification for neonatal sepsis signs, but in a low-resource admission setting only partly ED-related.

Research: Latent class phenotyping restricted to admission-available pSBI signs, with external and internal-external validation across 21 hospitals.
